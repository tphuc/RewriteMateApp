Ask for clarification by saying what you understand, naming the missing detail, and asking one specific question. A clear question shows that you are trying to complete the work accurately; it does not need a long apology.

For written workplace requests, specificity matters: Indeed recommends briefly identifying the parts you understood so the other person can see exactly what needs clarification. [Its guide to clarifying questions](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/clarifying-questions) is useful context.

## Use the confirm, gap, question structure

1. **Confirm:** State the part you understand.
2. **Gap:** Name the detail that is unclear.
3. **Question:** Ask for the exact information you need.

> Hi [Name], I understand that the report should cover [topic] for [audience]. Could you confirm whether you would like [option A] or [option B] included? That will help me prepare the right version.

## Examples for work messages

### An unclear task

> Thanks, I can take this on. Could you clarify what “final version” means here: should I make the edits, or send it for approval first?

### A vague deadline

> I understand this is a priority. Could you confirm the deadline you need me to work toward so I can plan the next step correctly?

### A client request

> I want to make sure I have understood your feedback correctly. Are you asking us to revise the homepage only, or the full set of pages?

### After a meeting

> My understanding is that I will prepare the draft and [Name] will review it. Could you confirm the intended audience and due date before I start?

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Try instead |
| --- | --- |
| “I do not understand.” | “Could you clarify what you mean by [specific point]?” |
| “What do you mean?” | “Could you share an example of the outcome you have in mind?” |
| “Please explain again.” | “Could you confirm whether [your interpretation] is correct?” |

Do not pretend to understand a requirement that affects scope, cost, timing, safety, policy, or a client commitment. Ask early, then record the answer in the appropriate work system.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it rude to ask for clarification at work?

No. A focused question helps prevent wrong work and makes the next step clear.

### How do I ask without sounding insecure?

Focus on the task, not on an apology: say what you understand and ask for the missing detail.

### Should I ask in Slack or email?

Use the normal project channel for a simple question. Use email or a documented system when the answer changes scope, cost, timing, or responsibility.
