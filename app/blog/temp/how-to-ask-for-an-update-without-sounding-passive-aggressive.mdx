# How to Ask for an Update Without Sounding Passive-Aggressive

Ask for an update by naming the work, explaining why you need it, and asking one clear question. Skip phrases that imply the other person has failed, such as “as per my last email” or “I am still waiting.”

The goal is not to make the request vague. A clear request is usually kinder because it gives the recipient enough context to reply quickly. The phrase “just checking in” is often intended to soften a follow-up, but it can sound passive-aggressive when it is used as a substitute for a direct question. [Scribbr’s guide to “just checking in”](https://www.scribbr.com/effective-communication/just-checking-in/) explains why the phrase can carry that unintended tone.

## Use the context, need, and next-step structure

Write three short parts:

1. **Context:** Identify the project, request, or earlier decision.
2. **Need:** State what you need in order to move forward.
3. **Next step:** Ask one answerable question or propose a realistic date.

For example:

> Hi Maya, I am preparing the client update for Thursday and need the latest approval status for the homepage copy. Is it ready to share, or should I note that approval is still in progress?

This is direct without assigning blame. It makes the requested reply easy: ready, not ready, or a different status.

## Messages to avoid

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| “Just checking in on this.” | “Could you share the current status of [project]?” |
| “As per my last email…” | “I am following up on [specific request] because [next action].” |
| “I still have not heard back.” | “I need [detail] to complete [task]. Is it available by [date]?” |
| “When can I expect an update?” | “Could you let me know whether [decision] is confirmed?” |

Use a firmer version when a deadline genuinely matters. Do not hide an urgent dependency behind a vague “checking in.”

## Copy and adapt these examples

### For a teammate in Slack

> Hi [Name], I am putting together [deliverable] today. Could you share the current status of [task] by [time], or let me know if the timeline has changed?

### For a client

> Hi [Name], following up on [item] so we can plan the next step. Could you confirm whether you would like us to proceed with [option], or share any feedback by [date]?

### For a manager or approver

> Hi [Name], I need your decision on [item] before I send [deliverable]. Are you comfortable approving it as is, or would you prefer a revision first?

### After a missed agreed date

> Hi [Name], we had planned to review [item] by [date]. I am checking where things stand so I can update [stakeholder] accurately. Is there a revised timing I should use?

## Match the request to the relationship

With a peer, name the shared work and ask for what you need. With a client, connect the request to their decision or next milestone. With someone who manages you, be concise and explain the dependency. In every case, avoid inventing urgency. If the recipient has not been given a deadline, do not write as if they missed one.

Good timing matters too. Follow the expectations already set for the project, and avoid sending repeated messages that ask the same unanswered question. If the request has changed, say what changed.

## Before you send

- Is the project or request named?
- Does the recipient know why you need the answer?
- Is there one easy question to answer?
- Did you remove blame and implied failure?
- Are dates and dependencies accurate?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it rude to ask for an update?

No. A direct request is reasonable when you need information to complete work. Keep it specific and proportionate to the timing already agreed.

### What is a better alternative to “just checking in”?

Name the subject and ask the actual question, such as “Could you share the current status of the proposal?”

### How do I ask for an update when the deadline passed?

Refer to the original date neutrally, explain the dependency, and ask for a revised timing rather than accusing the person of being late.

### Should I include a deadline?

Include one when it is real and explain the dependency it supports. Do not create an artificial deadline only to make the request feel more urgent.
