30 blog posts, keyword mapping, search intent, and internal linking structure

1) SEO strategy summary

Core positioning to build the blog around

RewriteMate should not try to compete broadly for generic terms like “AI writer” or “writing assistant.” The better play is to own a more specific category:

* AI keyboard for iPhone
* rewrite text inside any app
* mobile Grammarly alternative
* AI writing workflow without copy-paste
* custom AI commands for repetitive writing tasks

SEO goal

Use blog content to capture traffic from 4 buckets of intent:

1. Category discovery
    People searching for AI keyboards, grammar keyboards, or mobile writing tools.
2. Comparison / replacement intent
    People evaluating Grammarly, QuillBot, Apple Writing Tools, or ChatGPT workflows.
3. Workflow / problem-solving intent
    People trying to rewrite emails, paraphrase messages, translate text, or fix tone on iPhone.
4. Persona-specific intent
    Students, non-native English speakers, marketers, founders, and support teams who want help writing faster and better on mobile.

10) Editorial guidance for how these posts should feel

To make this content actually work for RewriteMate, the posts should consistently frame the problem as writing friction, not just “bad writing.”

Lean into these themes

* no more copy-paste
* improve text where you’re already typing
* rewrite inside any app
* keep your flow while writing
* fix tone, grammar, and clarity in one tap
* custom AI prompts for recurring writing tasks
* privacy-conscious writing help on iPhone

Avoid overly broad AI writing language like

* “generate amazing content in seconds”
* “supercharge your productivity with AI”
* “unlock the power of writing”

RewriteMate is more believable when it sounds specific, practical, calm, and workflow-aware.
